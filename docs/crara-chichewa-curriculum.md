# CRARA Chichewa learning sequence

## Purpose
Build a staged, source-grounded Chichewa capability for CRARA. This is a curriculum and retrieval resource, not a claim of model fine-tuning. The response-language preference is supplied separately by the CRARA interface.

## Source register
1. **Chichewa learning/conversation guide** — user-provided PDF, version 4, February 2011, 62 pages. Use as an introductory phrase and grammar aid only. It contains inconsistent spacing/spelling and mismatched quiz answers; do not treat every example as authoritative.
2. **Chinyanja (Chicewa)–English Dictionary** — user-provided PDF, originally compiled in 1979 and revised/republished in 1999, 72 pages. Use as a historical vocabulary cross-reference. Check regional, dated, spelling and meaning issues before teaching an entry as current standard Malawian Chichewa.
3. **Malawi Institute of Education, Chichewa syllabus, Forms 1–2** — 2013 edition, 139 pages. Publicly accessible copy: https://edu.wtec1.com/pdfs/Chichewa%20syllabus%20Forms%201-2.pdf
4. **Malawi Institute of Education, Chichewa syllabus, Forms 3–4** — 2013 edition, 153 pages. Publicly accessible copy: https://edu.wtec1.com/pdfs/Chichewa%20syllabus%20Forms%203-4.pdf
5. **Malawi Institute of Education, Curriculum and Assessment Framework for Schools and Colleges in Malawi** — July 2025 framework, available from https://mie.edu.mw/downloads/. Use as current high-level curriculum context; do not assume it replaces the subject syllabuses without checking an approved updated Chichewa syllabus.

## Progressive curriculum

### Stage 0 — Orientation and standard orthography
- Identify Chichewa as the target language and distinguish standard Malawian Chichewa from related Chinyanja labels and regional usage.
- Establish the standard written forms, vowel sounds, consonant sounds, syllables, stress and punctuation from vetted Chichewa teaching references.
- Teach the sound/spelling contrasts explicitly identified in the MIE secondary syllabus: **L/R, Dz/Z, Ts/S**.
- Introduce common consonant combinations and syllable construction only after checking them against reliable Chichewa orthography references.
- Do not simply impose English alphabet pronunciation rules on Chichewa.

### Stage 1 — First words and everyday communication
- Greetings and culturally appropriate responses.
- Names, introductions, family, people, places, numbers, dates and time.
- Polite requests, thanks, apologies, directions and common classroom instructions.
- Listening and speaking practice with short, natural dialogues.
- Correct spelling and word boundaries; explain variant forms where genuinely accepted.

### Stage 2 — Vocabulary and word classes
- Nouns and noun classes; singular/plural patterns and concord.
- Pronouns and demonstratives.
- Descriptive words, qualifiers, adverbs and connectors.
- High-frequency verbs and infinitive forms.
- Semantic distinctions, synonyms, antonyms, collocations and context-sensitive meanings.

### Stage 3 — Sentence construction and core grammar
- Natural Chichewa word order and agreement.
- Subject markers and object markers in verbs.
- Affirmative and negative clauses.
- Questions and interrogatives.
- Commands, requests and polite forms.
- Possession, location, comparison and description.
- Simple, compound and complex sentences.
- Punctuation and paragraph cohesion.

### Stage 4 — Verb morphology and time/aspect
- Present/progressive and habitual meanings.
- Past and future forms.
- Subject concord and object infixes.
- Verb extensions and derivation, including causative patterns where supported.
- Negation across relevant constructions.
- Distinguish grammatical rules from simplified examples in the 2011 guide.

### Stage 5 — Listening, speaking, reading and writing
- Comprehension of spoken instructions, conversations and announcements.
- Conversation in formal and informal settings.
- Reading comprehension and vocabulary in context.
- Paragraphs, letters, messages, reports, narratives and descriptive writing.
- Summarising, paraphrasing and proofreading.

### Stage 6 — Malawi secondary-school syllabus alignment (Forms 1–2)
Use the MIE syllabus scope and sequence rather than inventing a parallel examination curriculum. Cover the five language skills and the grammar areas in the scope/sequence, including:
- types, functions and classes of nouns;
- types and functions of pronouns;
- types of modifiers/descriptive words and other word classes;
- verb tenses;
- orthographic contrasts L/R, Dz/Z and Ts/S;
- adverbs;
- gender and singular/plural distinctions;
- sentence types and sentence construction.
Include oral and written literature: proverbs, riddles, short and long narratives, drama, songs, poetry and biography as specified in the syllabus.

### Stage 7 — Malawi secondary-school syllabus alignment (Forms 3–4)
Build on the Forms 1–2 foundation with more complex language use, listening and speaking in culturally appropriate contexts, messages and announcements, formal communication, advanced grammar, and close reading/analysis of oral and written genres. Include poetry, short and long stories, drama, literary devices, themes, characterisation, setting, plot and critical interpretation in line with the MIE scope and sequence.

### Stage 8 — Advanced and professional communication
- Academic explanations, argumentation, evidence and critical discussion.
- Formal letters, reports, meeting minutes, announcements and professional correspondence.
- Health, maternal and neonatal health, research, statistics and scientific vocabulary.
- Explain technical concepts in fluent Chichewa while retaining internationally recognised terms, abbreviations, formulae and units when translation would reduce precision.
- Distinguish a standard Chichewa translation from an explanatory paraphrase or borrowed English term.

### Stage 9 — Mastery checks and continuous improvement
- Short diagnostic tests before advancing.
- Listening, speaking, reading, writing and grammar checks.
- Translation in both directions, paraphrase, correction and open-ended composition.
- Track uncertain vocabulary and grammar separately; do not promote an unverified guess into the trusted corpus.
- Add reviewed corrections and examples as versioned learning data.

## Source-grounding rules
- The MIE Forms 1–2 and Forms 3–4 syllabuses define the secondary-school scope and sequence. They include oral/written literature and language skills, not just grammar.
- The 2011 guide and 1979/1999 dictionary are supplementary, not controlling authorities.
- Do not reproduce substantial passages from copyrighted syllabuses or dictionaries. Store short factual curriculum metadata, original teaching explanations and brief reviewed examples.
- For disputed or uncertain language, state uncertainty, retain the technical English term if needed, and request review by a fluent Malawian Chichewa speaker.
