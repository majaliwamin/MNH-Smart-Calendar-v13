# Chichewa — Lesson 1: Alphabet, vowels and first sounds

**Level:** Beginner  
**Learning goal:** Recognise the basic Chichewa letter sequence, pronounce the five vowel sounds consistently, and begin reading simple syllables and words.

## 1. The basic letter sequence

A Ministry of Education teacher-training resource reproduced in the USAID document identifies 24 letters in the Chichewa alphabet and gives this curriculum sequence, with vowels first:

**a, e, i, o, u, b, c, d, f, g, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, v, w, y, z**

The same resource also records a different early-literacy order (anikumeto), arranged by frequency of use, in which **ch** appears as one of the 24 introduced sound units. CRARA must not mix the two teaching sequences: use the vowel-first sequence above when presenting the basic alphabet, and explain **ch** separately as a two-letter spelling used for a distinct sound.

The user-provided 2011 phrase/grammar guide does not provide a dependable complete alphabet list. It is therefore not used as the authority for the alphabet.

## 2. The five vowels

Chichewa has five vowel sounds:

| Letter | Pronunciation guide | Examples in the Peace Corps/Malawi lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **a** | Similar to the vowel in *father* | *kaya*, *ana* |
| **e** | Similar to the vowel in *made* | *bwera*, *chemwali* |
| **i** | Similar to the vowel in *easy* | *ichi*, *liti* |
| **o** | Similar to the vowel in *only* | *moni*, *zikomo* |
| **u** | Similar to the vowel in *food* | *uchi*, *ulimi* |

These English comparisons are approximations for beginners. Learners should aim for clear, steady vowel sounds rather than English diphthongs.

## 3. Sound combinations to notice

The Peace Corps/Malawi introductory lesson specifically highlights these difficult combinations:

- **-ng'o-** — example: *pang'ono* (little).
- **-ng'a-** — example: *yang'ana* (look).
- **-psya** — example: *kuwopsya* (to be dangerous).

The secondary-school curriculum also draws attention to sound/spelling distinctions **L/R, Dz/Z and Ts/S**. These require listening and speaking practice; do not teach them as interchangeable.

## 4. Read the words aloud

Use these examples from the Peace Corps/Malawi introductory materials:

- *ana*
- *bwera*
- *ichi*
- *liti*
- *moni*
- *zikomo*
- *uchi*
- *ulimi*
- *pang'ono*
- *yang'ana*

Read each word slowly, keep the vowels clear, then repeat at a natural speaking pace. A fluent Malawian Chichewa speaker should review pronunciation where audio is not available.

## 5. First practice

1. Say the five vowels in order: **a — e — i — o — u**.
2. Read the alphabet sequence aloud, slowly.
3. Read the ten practice words.
4. Identify which vowel sound you hear in *ana*, *ichi*, *moni* and *uchi*.
5. Practise the three highlighted combinations: *pang'ono*, *yang'ana*, *kuwopsya*.

### Self-check

- How many basic vowel sounds does Chichewa have?

## 6. Answer key

1. Five.
2. **i**.
3. **ch**.
4. **L/R, Dz/Z and Ts/S**.

## Source notes and limitations

- **Alphabet and vowel-first sequence:** Ministry of Education, Science and Technology teacher-training material, *Practising Chichewa Letter Sounds*, reproduced in the USAID-hosted resource (the source states 24 letters and lists the vowel-first sequence).
- **Vowel examples and difficult combinations:** Peace Corps/Malawi, *Pre-departure Chichewa Language Materials*, Lesson 1.
- **Secondary curriculum scope:** Malawi Institute of Education, *Chichewa Syllabus Forms 1–2* (2013 edition retrieved for this project), which identifies the L/R, Dz/Z and Ts/S contrasts.
- **User-provided *chichewa.pdf*:** useful for later grammar topics (sentence structure, pronouns, verb prefixes/infixes, tense and negation), but its quiz and examples contain mismatches and inconsistent spelling. Do not use those questionable examples as the answer key.
- **User-provided *Chinyanja (Chicewa)–English Dictionary*:** historical vocabulary reference, not a sole modern orthographic authority.

This is a beginner lesson assembled from the cited sources, not a claim that the 2013 secondary syllabus itself begins with this exact lesson order.
