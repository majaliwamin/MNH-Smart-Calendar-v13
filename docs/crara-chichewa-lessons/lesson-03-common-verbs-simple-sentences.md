## 2. Examples of simple verb phrases in the grammar notes

The guide gives these examples on pages 40–42:

| Ndi ku dya | I am eating |

The source also prints **Ndi-ku-funa** for “I want” on page 40. Notice that its hyphenation and spacing vary between sections. Keep that inconsistency visible when explaining the source; do not pretend the PDF uses one consistent spelling convention.

## 3. A first look at time and aspect

On page 42, the guide presents these patterns using the verb **-pita**:

| Meaning described in the guide | Pattern printed | Example gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present activity | Ti-ku-pita | We are going |
| Habitual action | Ti-ma-pita | We go |
| Past | Ti-na-pita | We went |
| Future | Ti-dza-pita | We will go |

These are the guide's introductory patterns. The source has inconsistent spacing and some grammar explanations that need confirmation with a fluent Chichewa speaker or a reliable contemporary grammar before CRARA generalises them to every verb and subject.

## 4. What the guide says about verb structure

The grammar notes on page 42 explain that a verb in a sentence may contain a **prefix, infix or suffix**. They give examples:

- **Subject-related prefixes:** ndi-ku-dya (“I am eating”); zi-patso zi-ku-gwa (“the fruits are falling”).
- **Tense infixes:** Ti-ma-pita (“We go”); Ti-na-pita (“We went”); Ti-dza-pita (“We will go”).
- **Suffix examples:** -on-a (“to see”) and -one-tsa (“to show”); -gul-a (“to buy”) and -guli-tsa (“to sell”).

This is an introduction only. Do not infer a complete conjugation rule from these few examples.

## 5. Guided practice

Use only the forms and glosses printed in the lesson.

### Answer key

1. ku-dya
2. ku-mwa
3. Ndi ku pita

5. Ti-dza-pita
6. Prefix, infix and suffix.

## 6. Source-quality and teaching note

The supplied February 2011 guide is a useful beginner reference but contains inconsistent hyphenation, spacing and some questionable examples. In particular, the first-person column on page 24 is not consistently formatted, and the grammar notes use more than one way of writing the same forms.

Therefore CRARA should:

- identify these as forms **printed in the supplied guide**;
- preserve the source's wording when asked what the PDF says;
- avoid presenting every printed form as a verified contemporary standard;
- distinguish the infinitive list from sentence examples and tense patterns;
- avoid generating additional conjugations by analogy until verified;
- invite correction from a fluent Malawian Chichewa speaker where a form is uncertain.
