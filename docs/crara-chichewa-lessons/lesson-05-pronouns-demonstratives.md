# Chichewa — Lesson 5: Personal pronouns and demonstratives

**Level:** Beginner to lower-intermediate  
**Learning goal:** Recognise the common independent personal pronouns and use a small set of demonstratives to point to people and things.

## 1. Personal pronouns

A personal pronoun refers to a person or group without repeating their name or noun.

| Chichewa | English |
| --- | --- |
| ine | I / me |
| iwe | you (one person, familiar) |
| iye | he / she |
| ife | we / us |
| inu | you (plural or respectful singular) |
| iwo | they / them |

These are independent pronoun forms. They are not the same thing as the short subject markers that appear inside or alongside verbs.

## 2. Independent pronouns and subject markers are different

The supplied 2011 guide lists short forms alongside the pronouns (for example, **ine — ndi**, **iwe — u**, **ife — ti**, **inu — mu**). These short forms are grammatical markers used in sentence construction; they should not be memorised as interchangeable translations of the full pronouns.

For example, the guide prints:

- **Ine ndi Mavuto.** — I am Mavuto.
- **Iye ndi mulimi.** — He is a farmer.
- **Inu ndani?** — Who are you?
- **Ife tili pano.** — We are here.

The guide's spelling and spacing are inconsistent. Treat these as introductory source examples, not a complete grammar table.

## 3. Demonstratives: pointing to a person or thing

A demonstrative is a word used to point out or identify someone or something.

The supplied guide includes these introductory examples:

| Chichewa | English meaning in the guide |
| --- | --- |
| mwana uyu | this child |
| ichi | this thing |
| icho | that thing |

Example sentences printed in the guide:

- **Mwana uyu ali pano.** — This child is here.
- **Ichi ndi chani?** — What is this?
- **Chita ichi!** — Do this!

The guide also prints **mwana uyu ali apo** with a different English distance gloss. Because that section is inconsistent, this lesson does not use it to teach a reliable near/far contrast.

## 4. Important agreement reminder

Demonstratives change according to the noun class and the meaning being expressed. Do not assume that **uyu**, **ichi** or **icho** can follow every noun. Learn a demonstrative together with a verified noun example.

This lesson introduces only a few forms. It is not a complete demonstrative or concord chart.

## 5. Guided practice

Answer using the lesson above.

1. Which pronoun means **I / me**?
2. Which pronoun means **we / us**?
3. Which pronoun can mean **he / she**?
4. What does **inu** mean?
5. Translate **Iye ndi mulimi**.
6. What does **Ichi ndi chani?** ask?
7. In **Mwana uyu ali pano**, which words mean “this child”?
8. Are **ine** and **ndi** interchangeable in every sentence?

### Answer key

1. ine
2. ife
3. iye
4. you (plural or respectful singular)
5. He is a farmer.
6. What is this?
7. mwana uyu
8. No. **Ine** is an independent pronoun; **ndi** is a short grammatical form used in particular sentence patterns.
