# Chichewa — Lesson 6: Asking questions

**Level:** Beginner to lower-intermediate  
**Learning goal:** Recognise common question words, understand simple questions and give short, relevant answers.

## 1. Why question words matter

Question words help us ask for a person's identity, an object's name or description, a location, a reason or a quantity. Learn each word with a complete example rather than memorising it in isolation.

## 2. Common question words in the supplied guide

| Chichewa form | Meaning | Source note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ndani?** | who? | Printed in the guide's key-word list and question examples. |
| **chani?** | what? | Printed in the key-word list and examples such as *Chani ichi/icho?* |
| **kuti?** | where? | Printed in the key-word list and location questions. |
| **chifukwa?** | why? / because | Listed as “Why?” in the opening phrase list; its use in a full sentence needs more examples. |
| **zingati?** | how much? / how many? | Appears in the price questions; meaning depends on context. |

The guide also contains **liti?** in a damaged/mismatched mini-quiz on page 33. Do not teach that quiz line as a reliable model sentence. Confirm the form and its use with a current Malawian Chichewa grammar or fluent speaker before adding it to the core vocabulary.

## 3. Learn through complete examples

The following examples are transcribed from the supplied February 2011 guide. Its spacing and spelling are inconsistent, so they are source examples for recognition and discussion, not a complete grammar rule.

### Asking who?

- **Pali ndani pano?** — Who is here?
- **Mkazi uyo ndi ndani?** — Who is that woman?
- **Inu ndani?** — Who are you?

Possible short answer printed in the guide:

- **Ife tili pano.** — We are here.

### Asking what?

- **Chani ichi/icho?** — What is this/that?
- **U/Mu funa chani?** — What do you want?
- **Ichi ndi chani?** — What is this?

A useful answer pattern from the guide:

- **Ndi bukhu.** — It is a book.

### Asking where?

- **Chili kuti?** — Where is it?
- **Chimbudzi chili kuti?** — Where is the toilet?
- **Miguel ali kuti?** — Where is Miguel?

Location words used in the same source section:

- **pano** — here
- **apo** — there

Example answer:

- **Chimbudzi chili apo.** — The toilet is over there.

### Asking why?

The opening phrase list gives **chifukwa?** for “why?”. The supplied guide does not provide a sufficiently clear full question-and-answer pair for this word in context. Learn the meaning, but do not invent a sentence pattern from the isolated entry.

### Asking about quantity or price

- **Zingati?** — How much?
- **Bukhu ndi ndalama zingati?** — How much is the book?
- **Ma dollar asanu.** — Five dollars.

The guide's examples use money and prices. In other contexts, confirm whether the intended question is “how much” or “how many”.

## 4. A simple method for answering

When you hear a question:

1. Identify the question word.
2. Work out whether the speaker asks about a person, thing, place, reason or quantity.
3. Answer only the information requested.
4. If you do not understand, ask the speaker to repeat or explain; do not guess.

Useful expressions already introduced in earlier lessons include:

- **Inde.** — Yes.
- **Iyayi.** — No (the guide prints this form; spelling varies in the document).
- The 2011 guide prints several inconsistent variants of “I do not know”. Do not teach one as a verified standard form until it has been checked against a contemporary source.

## 5. Guided practice

Translate the question words, then answer the comprehension questions.

1. What does **ndani?** mean?
2. What does **chani?** mean?
3. Which question word asks about a place?
4. What does **Pali ndani pano?** ask?
5. Translate **Chimbudzi chili kuti?**
6. What does **Zingati?** ask in the price examples?
7. Give the English meaning of **Ife tili pano.**
8. Why should the damaged **liti?** quiz line not be used as a model sentence yet?

### Answer key

1. Who?
2. What?
3. **kuti?**
4. Who is here?
5. Where is the toilet?
6. How much?
7. We are here.
8. The quiz line on page 33 has mismatched question-and-answer entries; it needs verification before it can be taught as a reliable example.

## 6. Teaching and accuracy notes

This lesson uses the user's supplied February 2011 *Chichewa from English* guide, especially pages 2, 5–6 and 19. The guide is useful for initial exposure but contains inconsistent spelling, spacing and some mismatched quiz entries. Preserve the printed form when explaining what the PDF says; do not silently normalise it or claim every example is authoritative contemporary Chichewa.

CRARA must not:
- treat **ndani**, **chani**, **kuti**, **chifukwa** and **zingati** as interchangeable;
- invent full sentence patterns for **chifukwa?** or **liti?** from isolated or corrupted entries;
- assume **zingati?** always means “how much” regardless of context;
- silently repair source examples without explaining the correction and checking it;
- invent answers when a source example is unclear.
