# Chichewa — Lesson 8: Adjectives and describing words

**Level:** Beginner to lower-intermediate  
**Learning goal:** Recognise a describing word and observe how its form changes in familiar noun phrases.

## 1. What is an adjective?

An adjective describes a noun. It can tell us about a quality, condition, size, colour or other feature.

English examples include:
- a good person;
- good people;
- a good book.

In Chichewa, the form used with a noun can change according to grammatical agreement. Do not assume that the English adjective can be translated by attaching one unchanged Chichewa form to every noun.

## 2. A familiar example: -bwino

Lesson 4 introduced these noun phrases:

| Chichewa | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| munthu wabwino | a good person |
| anthu abwino | good people |
| mwana wabwino | a good child |
| ana abwino | good children |
| chinthu chabwino | a good thing |
| zinthu zabwino | good things |
| buku labwino | a good book |
| mabuku abwino | good books |

These examples show the descriptive meaning “good” and visible changes in the form that accompanies different nouns.

For this small set, compare:
- **wabwino** with **munthu** and **mwana**;
- **abwino** with **anthu** and **ana**;
- **chabwino** with **chinthu**;
- **zabwino** with **zinthu**;
- **labwino** with **buku**;
- **abwino** with **mabuku**.

The examples are a learning set, not a complete chart of adjective agreement.

## 3. Learn the noun and its agreeing description together

When learning a new adjective, practise it inside a complete noun phrase. For each example, record:

1. the noun and its meaning;
2. the noun's singular or plural form;
3. the complete adjective form used with that noun;
4. the meaning of the whole phrase;
5. the source or reference that confirms the agreement.

Do not take one form from this table and apply it to a noun that has not been checked.

## 4. Do not confuse different kinds of agreement

Lesson 4 also showed subject markers in verbs, such as **chi-** in **Chinthu chikugwa** and **zi-** in **Zinthu zikugwa**.

Those are verb subject-marker examples. They are not an adjective chart. CRARA must keep noun prefixes, adjective agreement and verb subject markers distinct when explaining grammar.

## 5. Guided practice

1. What does **munthu wabwino** mean?
2. Which phrase means “good people”: **munthu wabwino** or **anthu abwino**?
3. Complete the familiar phrase: **chinthu ______** (“a good thing”).
4. Which phrase means “good books”: **buku labwino** or **mabuku abwino**?
5. In **mwana wabwino**, identify the noun and the describing form.
6. Are **chi-** and **zi-** in **chikugwa/zikugwa** adjective forms?
7. Why should a learner not use **wabwino** with every noun?

### Answer key

1. A good person.
2. **anthu abwino**.
3. **chabwino**.
4. **mabuku abwino**.
5. **mwana** is the noun; **wabwino** is the agreeing descriptive form.
6. No. They are subject markers in the verb examples.
7. The form in the noun phrase changes with grammatical agreement; each new noun phrase must be verified.

## 6. Teaching and source-quality note

This lesson reuses the limited adjective-agreement examples documented in Lesson 4 and the supplied introductory guide. It does not claim that **-bwino** represents every adjective or that the displayed forms cover every noun class.

CRARA must not:
- present this table as a complete adjective-concord paradigm;
- assume every adjective follows exactly the same pattern;
- confuse adjective agreement with verb subject markers;
- generate unverified noun phrases by analogy;
- silently change source spellings when quoting the supplied guide.

For wider teaching, verify each new adjective and noun-class combination against a reliable contemporary grammar or a fluent Malawian Chichewa speaker.
