# Lesson 9: Adverbs and expressions of time, place and manner

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain what an adverb does, recognise expressions of time, place and manner, and distinguish an adverbial expression from an adjective that describes a noun.

## 1. What is an adverb?

An adjective describes a noun: it tells us more about a person, animal, place or thing. An adverb or adverbial expression gives information about an action or situation. It may tell us:

- **When?** — time
- **Where?** — place
- **How?** — manner

In English, “She arrived **today**,” “He is waiting **here**,” and “They spoke **slowly**” contain time, place and manner information respectively.

A word can have different grammatical functions depending on how it is used. Do not classify a Chichewa word as an adverb in every context simply because it appears in the guide's mixed “Adjectives/adverbs/Other” vocabulary list.

## 2. Expressions of time — when?

The supplied February 2011 guide lists these forms:

| Chichewa form as printed | Meaning in the guide |
|---|---|
| **lero** | today |
| **mawa** | tomorrow |
| **tsopano** | now |
| **madzulo** | in the evening |
| **posachedwa** | soon |
| **patsogolo** | later |
| **nthawi zonse** | always |

The guide also lists **nthawi** (time), **tsiku** (day) and **mphindi** (minute).

**Notice:** *lero* and *mawa* locate an event in time; *tsopano* points to the present moment; *madzulo* identifies a time of day. *posachedwa* and *patsogolo* both refer to a later time, but should not be assumed interchangeable in every sentence.

## 3. Expressions of place — where?

The guide lists:

| Chichewa form as printed | Meaning in the guide |
|---|---|
| **pano** | here |
| **apo** | there |
| **pafupi** | near |
| **patali** | far |

These expressions help locate a person, object or action. Compare “here” with “there”, or “near” with “far”.

The guide also uses **kuti?** in location questions and gives a printed “Where is the …?” pattern as *ndi kuti/pati*. Because the source's spacing and wording are inconsistent, learn the vocabulary first; do not treat that printed pattern as a complete question-construction rule.

## 4. Expressions of manner — how?

The guide's vocabulary section lists:

| Chichewa form as printed | Meaning in the guide |
|---|---|
| **mofulumira** | fast/quickly |
| **pang’ono pang’ono** | slowly |
| **-bwino** | well/good, depending on use |
| **-ipa** | badly/bad, depending on use |

The daily phrase section separately lists *pang’ono pang’ono* for “slowly”. Its printed full phrase for “Please speak louder” is not a model for “speak slowly”; do not combine the two into a sentence.

### Adjective or adverb?

The guide places *-bwino* and *-ipa* under a combined heading for “Adjectives/adverbs/Other”. Their English glosses include both adjective-like and adverb-like meanings:

- An adjective describes a **noun**: “a good book”.
- An adverbial use describes **how an action is done**: “speak well”.

Do not assume that an English gloss alone establishes the Chichewa grammatical category. The adjective agreement examples from Lesson 8 (for example, *buku labwino*) describe nouns; they are not a universal adverb pattern.

## 5. Guided identification

| Expression | Type of information | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **lero** | Time | today |
| **pano** | Place | here |
| **mofulumira** | Manner | fast/quickly |
| **madzulo** | Time | in the evening |
| **patali** | Place | far |
| **pang’ono pang’ono** | Manner | slowly |

These are vocabulary identifications, not newly constructed Chichewa sentences.

## 8. Source and accuracy note

This lesson draws on the user's supplied *Chichewa from English*, Version 4, February 2011:

- Page 25: the mixed “Adjectives/adverbs/Other” list, including *-bwino*, *-ipa*, *tsopano*, *patsogolo*, *pang’ono pang’ono* and *pano/apo*.
- Pages 26–27: *mofulumira*, *nthawi zonse*, *lero*, *mawa*, *patsogolo*, *tsiku*, *nthawi* and related vocabulary.
- Pages 31–32: examples involving *pang’ono pang’ono*, *lero*, *mawa*, *madzulo*, *posachedwa*, *pano/apo* and *pafupi/patali*.

The 2011 guide contains inconsistent spelling, spacing and grammatical labelling. This lesson preserves forms as printed and does not silently correct them. The vocabulary meanings should be checked with a contemporary Chichewa reference or fluent Malawian speaker before being treated as definitive. No complete adverb paradigm or new Chichewa sentence pattern is inferred from this limited list.
