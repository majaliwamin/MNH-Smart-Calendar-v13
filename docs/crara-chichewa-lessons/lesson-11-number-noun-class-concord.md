# Lesson 11: Number and noun-class concord

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- distinguish singular and plural noun forms;
- recognise that a noun's class influences the forms that agree with it;
- trace the noun, descriptive word and verb subject marker in the small set of documented examples;
- avoid creating plural forms or concord markers by guesswork.

## 1. Number and noun class are related, but not identical

**Number** tells us whether we are referring to one item (singular) or more than one (plural). A **noun class** is a grammatical category that helps organise nouns and the agreement patterns associated with them.

A noun's plural is not always made by adding the same ending. Learn the singular and plural together as a pair, and learn the agreement forms that are actually used with that pair.

## 2. Review the documented singular/plural pairs

The following pairs were introduced in Lesson 4:

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **munthu** | **anthu** | person / people |
| **mwana** | **ana** | child / children |
| **chinthu** | **zinthu** | thing / things |
| **buku** | **mabuku** | book / books |

Notice that the forms change in different ways:
- **munthu → anthu**
- **mwana → ana**
- **chinthu → zinthu**
- **buku → mabuku**

These are learned pairs. They are not four interchangeable plural-making rules, and they do not allow us to predict every other noun's plural.

## 3. Concord inside a noun phrase

**Concord** means that another word changes its form in a way that corresponds to the noun class. Lesson 8 showed these documented descriptive phrases:

| Singular phrase | Plural phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **munthu wabwino** | **anthu abwino** | a good person / good people |
| **mwana wabwino** | **ana abwino** | a good child / good children |
| **chinthu chabwino** | **zinthu zabwino** | a good thing / good things |
| **buku labwino** | **mabuku abwino** | a good book / good books |

Compare the noun and the descriptive form together. For example, the noun changes from **munthu** to **anthu**, and the documented description changes from **wabwino** to **abwino**.

Do not treat **wa-**, **a-**, **cha-** or **la-** as a single prefix that can be attached to any noun. The examples show particular agreements, not a universal adjective formula.

## 4. Concord in the verb phrase

Lesson 4 also provided these introductory statements:

| Sentence as taught in Lesson 4 | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Munthu akudya.** | The person is eating. |
| **Anthu akudya.** | The people are eating. |
| **Chinthu chikugwa.** | The thing is falling. |
| **Zinthu zikugwa.** | The things are falling. |

In these examples, the noun and the verb form belong together. The human singular/plural pair is shown with **a-** in this particular verb pattern. The thing/things pair is shown with **chi-** and **zi-** respectively.

This is a deliberately small comparison. It is not a complete subject-marker chart, and the examples do not establish how every verb behaves in every tense or aspect.

## 5. A simple method for analysing concord

When you meet a new sentence, work through it in this order:

1. **Find the noun.** What person or thing is being discussed?
2. **Identify its number.** Is the noun singular or plural?
3. **Check the noun pair.** Do you know its verified partner?
4. **Look at the description.** Does an adjective or other modifier agree with the noun?
5. **Look at the verb.** Is there a subject marker, and is its use supported by a reliable example?
6. **Record the whole example.** Do not learn a marker detached from the noun and sentence where it occurs.

### B. Complete the documented pairs

- **buku labwino**
- **mabuku abwino**

## 7. Answer guide

**A. Number**

1. **munthu** — singular.
2. **ana** — plural.
3. **zinthu** — plural.
4. **mabuku** — plural.

**B. Pairs**

1. anthu
2. ana
3. zinthu
4. mabuku

**C. Descriptive concord**

**Mabuku abwino** means “good books”. The noun changes from **buku** to **mabuku**, and the documented descriptive form changes from **labwino** to **abwino**.

**D. Verb examples**

1. **Chinthu** is the noun; **chi-** is the subject marker in **chikugwa**.
2. **Zinthu** is the noun; **zi-** is the subject marker in **zikugwa**.
3. The noun changes from singular **chinthu** to plural **zinthu**, and the subject marker changes from **chi-** to **zi-** in these examples.

## 8. Source and accuracy note

This lesson consolidates the limited examples already introduced in the user's supplied February 2011 guide and the curated CRARA Lessons 4 and 8. It does not add new Chichewa vocabulary, new plural forms or new concord paradigms.

The source guide has inconsistent spelling, spacing and grammatical explanations. The examples are retained as the forms taught in the earlier lessons, not presented as a complete modern reference. Do not infer a noun's class from its first letter, create a plural by analogy, or generalise the adjective and subject-marker examples to all nouns and verbs. Verify additional class pairings and concord patterns with a contemporary Chichewa grammar or a fluent Malawian Chichewa speaker.

## Lesson 11 checkpoint

You should now be able to identify singular/plural pairs and observe how the descriptive form or verb subject marker changes in the documented examples. The next lesson can extend concord into other parts of the noun phrase, but only where reliable examples support the pattern.
